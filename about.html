Our story

Built one student at a time.

SriKeys Academy of Music started in a single room with an upright piano, six students and a conviction that music lessons should feel less like an assembly line and more like a conversation.

Since 2012

From six students to a community of musicians.

Sridevi Raman began teaching piano in Chennai in 2012, after years of accompanying choirs, soloists and school productions. Parents began to notice something: her students practised without being nagged, and they played with a confidence that went beyond their grade.

Word travelled through school corridors and WhatsApp groups. By 2016 the waiting list had outgrown the room, and SriKeys moved to its current studio in Adyar. In 2020, when lessons moved online, something unexpected happened — students joined from Singapore, Dubai, the United States and Australia, and SriKeys became a global classroom without losing the intimacy of a single teacher and a single student at the piano.

Today more than 180 students learn with us. Some are preparing for ABRSM Grade 8. Some are five years old and learning to keep a steady beat. Some are adults who always wanted to play and finally decided it was not too late. All of them receive the same thing: undivided attention.

Vision & mission

What we are building towards.

✧

Our vision

A generation of musicians who read, listen and express — for whom music is not a certificate on a wall but a lifelong companion. We want SriKeys to be the place a family recommends without hesitation, for the teaching and for the care.

♬

Our mission

To give every student a personal plan, an honest assessment of progress, and a stage to perform on. To teach technique with rigour, theory with purpose and expression with joy. And to keep every lesson small enough that no question goes unheard.

Philosophy

Four ideas we teach by.

These are not slogans. They shape how a lesson is planned, how homework is set and how progress is reported.

1

Understanding before speed

We would rather a student play a piece slowly and know why the harmony moves than play it fast by memory. Comprehension is what survives a forgotten week of practice.

2

Technique in service of music

Scales, arpeggios and studies are not a punishment. They are the vocabulary that lets a student phrase a melody the way they hear it in their head.

3

Performance is part of learning

A recital, a school assembly, a living room — every student performs. The first performance is terrifying; the tenth is a habit of confidence.

4

Parents are partners

Practice notes, attendance, homework and quarterly reports are visible to families in the student portal. Nothing about a child's progress should be a surprise in December.
